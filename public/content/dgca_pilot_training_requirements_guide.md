# Complete Guide to DGCA Requirements for Becoming a Commercial Pilot in India

Navigating the regulatory framework of the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) is the essential first step for anyone aiming to become a commercial pilot in India. As the statutory body under the Ministry of Civil Aviation, the DGCA governs civil aviation safety, airworthiness standards, and all flight crew licensing under the Indian Aircraft Rules, 1937. 

Meeting DGCA requirements demands a clear, step-by-step approach—from academic prerequisites and medical evaluations to ground school examinations and logging mandatory flight hours.

---

## Core Eligibility Criteria for DGCA Pilot Licenses

The DGCA specifies precise criteria based on the stage of flight training and the type of license sought.

### 1. Age Requirements
* **Student Pilot License (SPL):** Minimum **16 years** to obtain an SPL and initiate basic dual flight training.
* **Commercial Pilot License (CPL):** Minimum **18 years** on the date of application submission. Candidates may begin ground classes and flight training at age 17, but the license itself will only be issued upon reaching 18 years of age.
* **Upper Age Limit:** There is no official upper age limit to begin pilot training in India. However, active commercial privileges under a CPL or Airline Transport Pilot License (ATPL) are subject to age limits governed by airline recruitment policies and medical currency rules (up to age 65 for commercial air transport).

### 2. Educational Qualifications
* **Mandatory Subjects:** Candidates must pass Class 12 (10+2 pattern) or its equivalent from a recognized central or state board with **Physics and Mathematics**.
* **Minimum Marks:** The DGCA does not mandate a minimum percentage cutoff for Class 12; passing the board examinations in both Physics and Math is the legal requirement.
* **Non-Physics/Maths Students (NIOS Option):** Aspiring pilots who completed Class 12 without Physics or Mathematics can fulfill this requirement by taking On-Demand Examinations in these specific subjects through the National Institute of Open Schooling (NIOS).

### 3. Medical Fitness Standards
DGCA medical evaluations are strictly sequential and conducted by certified Aviation Medical Examiners (AME) or designated Air Force Medical Centers:
* **Class 2 Medical:** The initial assessment conducted by a DGCA-empaneled Medical Examiner. Passing this provides the medical assessment necessary for registering a DGCA Computer Number and issuing an SPL.
* **Class 1 Medical:** A comprehensive, rigorous physical and diagnostic evaluation (including ECG, EEG, audiometry, vision testing, and blood panels) conducted at designated military/civil aviation medical centers. A valid Class 1 Medical Certificate is mandatory before solo flights and for the final issuance of a CPL.
* **Vision Standards:** Visual acuity must be 6/6 in one eye and up to 6/9 in the other, correctable to 6/6 with glasses or contact lenses, provided color vision and refractive limits meet DGCA parameters.

## DGCA Pariksha Portal & Computer Number

To appear for any DGCA theory examination, candidates must register on the official eGCA / Pariksha portal to be assigned a unique **Computer Number**. 

**Key Requirements for Computer Number Allocation:**
1. Verification of Class 10 and Class 12 mark sheets (verified directly with the respective education boards).
2. Verified identity proof (Aadhaar, Passport).
3. Class 2 Medical Assessment file details.

## Mandatory DGCA Ground Written Examinations

To qualify for a Commercial Pilot License, candidates must pass theory examinations conducted by the DGCA Central Examination Organisation (CEO). All papers require a minimum score of **70%** to pass.

| Exam Paper | Subject Scope |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Air Navigation** | Chart work, dead reckoning, flight planning, radio navigation aids, instrument systems, and flight performance. |
| **Aviation Meteorology** | Atmospheric physics, weather hazards, pressure systems, METAR/TAF interpretation, monsoon patterns, and thunderstorm safety. |
| **Air Regulations** | Indian Aircraft Rules 1937, Civil Aviation Requirements (CARs), ICAO Annexes, air law, and airspace classifications. |
| **Technical General** | Principles of flight, aircraft structures, engines (piston/jet), electrical systems, hydraulics, and avionics. |
| **Technical Specific** | Technical operational data specific to the type of aircraft used during flight training (e.g., Cessna 172, Piper PA-28). |
| **Radio Telephony (RTR-A)** | Radio communication procedures conducted by the Wireless Planning & Coordination (WPC) Wing of the Ministry of Communications, with DGCA license endorsement. |

*Note: DGCA exams are held in sessions four times a year across major examination centers (including Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, and Hyderabad). Cleared paper results remain valid for a specific timeframe governed by DGCA CARs during flight logging.*

## Flight Training Requirements (200 Hours Minimum)

Under Schedule II, Section J of the Aircraft Rules 1937, candidates must log a minimum of **200 flying hours** at a DGCA-approved Flying Training Organisation (FTO) in India or an equivalent foreign authority-approved school (subject to DGCA license conversion rules).

The standard break-up of 200 required flying hours includes:
* **100 Hours** of Solo Flight Time.
* **50 Hours** of Cross-Country Flight Time (Solo and Dual combined).
* **40 Hours** of Instrument Flying (up to 20 hours permitted on a DGCA-approved flight simulator).
* **5 Hours** of Night Flight Time (including solo take-offs and landings).
* **CPL Skill Tests (Check Rides):** Practical tests with a DGCA-approved Flight Examiner evaluating general flying by day, general flying by night, and instrument rating skills.

## FAQs on DGCA Requirements

### Q1: Can I apply for a DGCA Computer Number before clearing Class 12?
No. The DGCA requires verified Class 10 and Class 12 mark sheets showing pass status in Physics and Mathematics before allocating a Computer Number.

### Q2: What happens if I failed Physics or Math in my school board exams?
You can clear Physics and Mathematics separately through the National Institute of Open Schooling (NIOS) On-Demand Examinations. The DGCA accepts NIOS certificates as fully equivalent to traditional state or central board credentials.

### Q3: How many attempts are allowed for DGCA theory exams?
There is no cap on the number of attempts permitted for DGCA written papers. You may re-appear in subsequent exam cycles until all subjects are cleared.

### Q4: Is a DGCA CPL valid internationally?
A DGCA-issued CPL permits you to fly Indian-registered (VT-registered) aircraft. To fly aircraft registered under other aviation authorities (such as the FAA or EASA), you must convert your license following that specific country's regulatory conversion process.
