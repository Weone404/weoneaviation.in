# DGCA Pilot Medicals: Class 2 & Class 1 Standards in India

Medical fitness is one of the foundational requirements for earning a Commercial Pilot License (CPL) or Private Pilot License (PPL) in India. The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) enforces strict medical standards to ensure that every pilot operating an aircraft meets the physical and mental health benchmarks required for safe flight operations.

For aspiring aviators, navigating the medical examination process is often the very first practical step toward a career in the cockpit. Understanding how DGCA medicals work—from your initial Class 2 assessment to the mandatory Class 1 examination—helps prevent costly delays and clarifies your eligibility before you invest heavily in ground training or flight hours.

---

## Overview of DGCA Medical Examinations

In India, medical assessments for civil aviation are divided into two primary categories for trainee and commercial pilots:

```
[ Prospective Trainee ]
          │
          ▼
┌───────────────────┐
│   Class 2 Medical │  Initial entry-level assessment (DGCA Empaneled Medical Examiner)
└─────────┬─────────┘
          │ (Issues Class 2 Assessment from DGCA)
          ▼
┌───────────────────┐
│   Class 1 Medical │  Mandatory for CPL issue & commercial flying (DGCA Medical Centres)
└───────────────────┘
```

1. **Class 2 Medical Examination:** The entry-level medical assessment. It is required prior to issuing a Student Pilot License (SPL) or PPL, and serves as the mandatory prerequisite before applying for a Class 1 medical.
2. **Class 1 Medical Examination:** The comprehensive medical fitness assessment required to hold and exercise the privileges of a Commercial Pilot License (CPL) and Airline Transport Pilot License (ATPL).

## DGCA Class 2 Medical Examination

The Class 2 medical assessment establishes a baseline of general health. It screens candidates for any pre-existing or underlying physical conditions that could interfere with aviation safety.

### Eligibility & Prerequisites
* **Minimum Age:** 16 years for SPL / 17 years for PPL or CPL enrolment pathway.
* **Educational Background:** No specific educational prerequisite is required solely to take a medical exam, but candidates pursuing a CPL must complete 10+2 with Physics and Mathematics.

### Medical Evaluation Parameters

| Health Parameter | Standard Requirement | Common Diagnostic Tests |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Visual Acuity** | Distance vision must be correctable to $6/6$ in each eye. Corrective lenses (glasses/contact lenses) are permitted within DGCA limits. | Snellen chart visual examination, refraction tests. |
| **Color Vision** | Normal color perception is mandatory for safe recognition of cockpit displays and aviation signal lights. | Ishihara color plates; lantern tests if Ishihara results are inconclusive. |
| **Hearing & ENT** | Ability to hear a quiet conversational voice at a distance of 2 meters with both ears. | Basic whisper/audiometric assessment, nasal and throat inspection. |
| **Cardiovascular Health** | Normal blood pressure standards and absence of structural heart abnormalities. | Electrocardiogram (ECG), resting heart rate and blood pressure checks. |
| **General Physical Health** | Normal systemic health (nervous system, gastrointestinal, respiratory, and musculoskeletal systems). | Blood tests (CBC, HbA1c/fasting blood sugar), urine routine analysis (protein/sugar screening). |

### Class 2 Application Process

1. **Create an eGCA Profile:** Register on the official DGCA eGCA portal (`egca.dgca.gov.in`) to generate a unique Pilot Identification (PID).
2. **Locate an Examiner:** Search the updated list of DGCA-empaneled Class 2 Medical Examiners available on the eGCA portal or DGCA official website.
3. **Book an Appointment:** Schedule an appointment directly with the empaneled Class 2 doctor.
4. **Complete Lab Tests:** Obtain required diagnostic lab reports (blood, urine, ECG, eye examination) prior to or as advised by the examiner.
5. **Medical Assessment:** Complete the physical examination. The examiner uploads your reports and medical assessment directly to the eGCA portal.
6. **DGCA Assessment Certificate:** The DGCA Medical Cell reviews the uploaded file and issues a formal Class 2 Assessment Sheet.

## DGCA Class 1 Medical Examination

Once an aspiring pilot completes their Class 2 medical and receives an official DGCA file number, they must undergo an Initial Class 1 Medical Examination. This exam is conducted at specialized DGCA Class 1 Medical Centres (primarily Indian Air Force Boarding Establishments and designated civil aviation hospitals).

### Key Clinical Components
The Class 1 exam involves rigorous multi-specialty evaluations:

* **Ophthalmology:** Detailed eye checkup, including fundoscopy, ocular motility, visual fields, and intraocular pressure.
* **ENT Evaluation:** Comprehensive ear, nose, and throat examination, including Pure Tone Audiometry (PTA) to test hearing frequency thresholds.
* **Internal Medicine:** In-depth evaluation of liver, kidney, cardiovascular, and metabolic health.
* **Psychiatry & Neurology:** Assessment of neurological stability, reflexes, and psychological readiness for commercial flight operations.
* **Radiology & Diagnostics:** Chest X-ray, abdominal ultrasonography (USG), full lipid profile, renal function tests, and baseline EEG (if clinically indicated).

### Class 1 Medical Centres in India
Initial Class 1 medicals are authorized at select military and civil centers across major aviation hubs in India, including:
* Institute of Aerospace Medicine (IAM), Bengaluru
* Air Force Central Medical Establishment (AFCME), New Delhi
* Boarding Centers at Air Force Stations in Mumbai, Jorhat, Tambaram, and Kanpur
* Designated Civil Class 1 Hospitals (as listed on the eGCA portal)

## Validity and Renewal Timelines

Commercial pilots must maintain active Class 1 medical status throughout their careers. Medical validity periods vary according to age and license type:

| Medical Class | Target Group / Age Bracket | Validity Period |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Class 2 Medical** | PPL / Trainees under 50 years | 60 months (5 years) |
| **Class 2 Medical** | PPL / Trainees 50 years and above | 24 months (2 years) |
| **Class 1 Medical** | Commercial Pilots under 60 years | 12 months (1 year) |
| **Class 1 Medical** | Commercial Pilots 60 years and above (or single-pilot ops over 40) | 6 months |

*Note: Renewals must be scheduled prior to expiry to prevent licensing suspension.*

## Common Medical Disqualifications & Temporary Unfitness

A finding of "Unfit" does not always mean the end of a pilot career. DGCA categorizes unfitness into two types:

```
DGCA Medical Result
 ├── Fit
 └── Unfit
      ├── Temporary Unfitness (Remediable conditions: high BP, overweight, vision issues)
      └── Permanent Unfitness (Severe chronic/progressive neurological or cardiac conditions)
```

1. **Temporary Unfitness:** Conditions that can be resolved with lifestyle changes, treatment, or minor corrective procedures (e.g., elevated blood pressure, temporary vision changes, overweight/BMI discrepancies, kidney stones, or minor surgical recovery). Candidates are given a specific window to re-examine after treatment.
2. **Permanent Unfitness:** Severe conditions that pose a risk to flight safety (e.g., uncontrolled epilepsy, significant cardiac pathology, severe color blindness).

### Appeal Process
If declared temporarily or permanently unfit, candidates have the right to appeal under DGCA Civil Aviation Requirements (CAR) Section 7, Series C, Part I. Appeals can be submitted to the Medical Cell at DGCA Headquarters for a review medical board or a special medical board at designated centers.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Can I become a pilot if I wear glasses?
Yes. Distant visual acuity must be correctable to $6/6$ in each eye using spectacle lenses or contact lenses, provided the refractive error falls within DGCA-permissible limits.

### How long does it take to get the Class 2 assessment sheet from DGCA?
After your Class 2 doctor uploads your medical documents via eGCA, the DGCA Medical Cell typically processes and issues the assessment within a few weeks, depending on document completeness and administrative workflow.

### Can I start ground classes before passing my Class 2 medical?
Yes, you can enroll in CPL ground classes to prepare for DGCA exams while your Class 2 medical process is underway. However, you must secure your Class 2 medical assessment prior to initiating Class 1 medical appointments and active flight training.

### What happens if I am declared Temporarily Unfit during my Class 1 medical?
You will receive a medical report detailing the reason for temporary unfitness along with a stipulated timeframe (e.g., 4 to 12 weeks) to treat or resolve the condition. Once resolved, you can schedule a re-examination at the designated center.
