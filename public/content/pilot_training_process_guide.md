Becoming a commercial pilot in India requires navigating a structured regulatory framework governed by the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA). From academic eligibility to earning your Commercial Pilot License (CPL), each step must be completed in a specific sequence to avoid unnecessary delays and expenses.

This guide breaks down the pilot training pathway step-by-step, outlining medical requirements, theoretical examinations, flight hours, and regulatory procedures.

## Overview of the CPL Pathway

The timeline to earn a Commercial Pilot License in India typically takes **18 to 24 months**. Training is divided into two main streams:
1. **Theoretical Knowledge (Ground School):** Clearing required DGCA written examinations and Radio Telephony (RTR) certification.
2. **Practical Flight Training:** Accumulating a minimum of 200 flight hours at a DGCA-approved Flying Training Organization (FTO).

## Step 1: Verification of Academic Eligibility

Before enrolling in any ground school or applying for medical checks, candidates must fulfill basic educational requirements set by the DGCA:

* **Minimum Age:** 17 years old to commence flight instruction; 18 years old for CPL issuance.
* **Education:** Passed 10+2 (or equivalent) with Physics and Mathematics from a recognized board.
* **Language Proficiency:** Functional English (reading, writing, and speaking).

> **Non-Science Graduates:** Candidates who completed 10+2 in Arts, Commerce, or Humanities without Physics or Mathematics can complete these individual subjects through the **National Institute of Open Schooling (NIOS)** On-Demand Examination system to qualify for DGCA registration.

---

## Step 2: DGCA Medical Examinations

Medical fitness is mandatory for all flight crew. The medical assessment happens in two distinct phases:

### Phase A: Class 2 Medical Examination
The Class 2 assessment is conducted by a designated DGCA Medical Examiner (Civil Medical Doctor). It covers general physical health, basic vision standards (corrected 6/6 vision in one eye, up to 6/9 in the other), hearing, blood chemistry, and an ECG. Passing this assessment generates a Medical Assessment certificate required to request a DGCA Computer Number.

### Phase B: Class 1 Medical Examination
The Class 1 assessment is stricter and takes place at an authorized Air Force Medical Establishment (e.g., IAM Bengaluru, AFCME New Delhi, or designated civilian centers). It evaluates cardiovascular health, neurological stability, ENT fitness, and detailed ophthalmological parameters. While you can start ground school with a Class 2 medical, obtaining a **Class 1 Medical Fitness Certificate is mandatory before CPL issuance**.

---

## Step 3: Application for a DGCA Computer Number

The **Computer Number** is a unique identifier issued to candidates via DGCA’s **eGCA portal**. It allows candidates to apply for written exams and track flight hours.

### Required Documents for eGCA Registration:
* Class 10 and 12 mark sheets (verified/attested)
* Class 2 Medical Assessment Certificate
* Government identification (Aadhaar / Passport)
* Passport-sized photographs adhering to DGCA dimensions

---

## Step 4: DGCA Ground School & Theoretical Examinations

A pilot must understand aircraft systems, operational rules, navigation techniques, and weather patterns. Ground school prepares candidates for the **six compulsory DGCA examinations**:

| Subject | Scope & Knowledge Covered | Passing Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Air Regulations** | Civil Aviation Requirements (CARs), air laws, right-of-way rules, operational limits, airspace classifications. | 70% minimum |
| **Air Navigation** | Flight planning, heading calculations, chart reading, dead reckoning, radio navigation, fuel burn rates. | 70% minimum |
| **Aviation Meteorology** | Weather physics, cloud formations, METAR/TAF report interpretation, jet streams, icing conditions. | 70% minimum |
| **Technical General** | Aircraft structures, flight controls, piston/jet engines, hydraulic systems, electrical systems. | 70% minimum |
| **Technical Specific** | Systems, performance specs, and emergency checks for the specific training aircraft (e.g., Cessna 172 or Piper Archer). | 70% minimum |
| **Radio Telephony (RTR-A)** | Communication protocols governed by the Wireless Planning & Coordination (WPC) wing. | Separate Practical / Viva exam |

---

## Step 5: Flight Training (200 Total Hours)

Once enrolled at a DGCA-approved Flying Training Organization (FTO), practical flight training begins. According to DGCA Aircraft Rules, student pilots must log a minimum of **200 flight hours**, categorized as follows:

* **100 Hours as Pilot-in-Command (PIC):** Solo flight time where the student acts as the sole commander.
* **50 Hours of Cross-Country Flight Time:** Flights landing at an airport other than the origin field.
* **20 Hours of Instrument Flying:** Flying solely by reference to cockpit instruments (simulated or actual conditions).
* **5 Hours of Night Flying:** Including night takeoffs, landings, and night cross-country flights.

---

## Step 6: Flight Skill Test & CPL Application

After accumulating 200 flight hours and passing all written exams, the candidate undergoes a **General Flying Test (GFT)** with a DGCA-approved flight examiner. The test assesses emergency handling, steep turns, instrument approaches, and cross-country navigation. 

Upon successful evaluation, the examiner submits the skill check report to DGCA along with flight logbooks, RTR license, and Class 1 medical, leading to the official issuance of the **Commercial Pilot License (CPL)**.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### 1. Can I become a pilot if I studied Commerce or Arts in 12th grade?
Yes. Candidates without Physics and Mathematics in 10+2 can write these two subjects through the **National Institute of Open Schooling (NIOS)** On-Demand Examinations. Once passed, you meet DGCA eligibility requirements.

### 2. Can I take DGCA ground exams before joining a flying school?
Yes. You can complete your Class 2 Medical, obtain a DGCA Computer Number, and pass all written ground school exams before committing to a flying school. Clearing exams first allows you to focus strictly on flight hours without study distractions during active flying.

### 3. What is the difference between Class 1 and Class 2 medicals?
A **Class 2 Medical** is an entry-level health assessment conducted by civil medical doctors. It is required to issue a DGCA Computer Number and Student Pilot License. A **Class 1 Medical** is a stricter evaluation conducted at authorized military/civilian medical boards and is required for commercial flying operations and CPL issuance.

### 4. How long does it take to complete the CPL process in India?
On average, the process takes **18 to 24 months**. Ground school and written exams typically require 3 to 6 months, while accumulating 200 flight hours takes 12 to 18 months, depending on weather and aircraft availability.
